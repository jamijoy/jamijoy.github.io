Case Study — Customer Segmentation

Turning transaction noise into groups the business could actually act on

Marketing was running the same campaign against every customer — regardless of whether they were a loyal repeat buyer or someone about to churn. I built an unsupervised model to split the customer base into behavioral segments the team could target directly, using nothing but existing transaction data.

Role
Data Scientist (solo)
Timeline
6 weeks
Dataset
86K customers · 2.1M orders
Stack
Python · scikit-learn · SQL

Methodology

How the model groups customers

A five-stage pipeline that turns raw order history into behavioral clusters, from cleaning to validated segments.

01
Data cleaning
Deduplicated orders, removed refund-only accounts, and resolved guest checkouts to unified customer IDs.
02
Feature engineering
Built RFM features — recency, frequency, monetary value — plus category diversity and discount sensitivity.
03
Scaling & PCA
Standardized features and reduced to 2 principal components to remove correlation and ease clustering.
04
K-Means clustering
Chose k=4 using the elbow method and silhouette score, then fit K-Means on the reduced feature space.
05
Profiling & validation
Labeled clusters by inspecting centroid behavior, and validated stability across three monthly re-runs.
Why K-Means over alternatives

Hierarchical clustering and DBSCAN were tested first, but with fairly uniform density and a business need for a fixed, explainable number of segments, K-Means gave the cleanest, most stable groupings — silhouette score of 0.58 versus 0.41 for DBSCAN on the same feature set.

Results

Four segments emerged from the data

Projected onto two principal components, the clusters separate cleanly — each with distinct purchase behavior the business could recognize immediately.

Next Steps

How the model could be improved

The initial model was built quickly to meet a business need, but there are several ways it could be enhanced for future iterations.

  • Incorporate more features, such as product categories and customer demographics, to improve cluster separation.
  • Test alternative clustering algorithms, such as Gaussian Mixture Models or Agglomerative Clustering, for potentially better results.
  • Implement a feedback loop with marketing to continuously refine segments based on campaign performance.
  • Automate the pipeline to update segments in real-time as new transaction data comes in.

Reflection

Lessons learned from the project

This project provided valuable insights into both the technical and business aspects of customer segmentation.

  • Understanding the business context is crucial for defining meaningful segments that can drive actionable marketing strategies.
  • Data quality and preprocessing play a significant role in the success of clustering algorithms; careful cleaning and feature engineering are essential.
  • Collaboration with stakeholders, such as marketing teams, ensures that the segments created are practical and aligned with business goals.
  • Continuous evaluation and iteration of the model are necessary to adapt to changing customer behaviors and market conditions.
